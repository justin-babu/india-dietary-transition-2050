# What if India Turned Vegetarian by 2050?

### An Undergraduate Economics Dissertation

## Overview

This dissertation investigates the economic feasibility of adopting a
completely vegan diet in India by 2050. It examines whether an individual
can meet specified nutritional requirements through a plant-based diet
while minimising dietary expenditure, and how its affordability may
change between 2020 and 2050.

The study draws on the relationship between food prices, nutritional
requirements, income and the potential environmental implications of
dietary transitions.

## Research Question

How economically feasible would a completely vegan diet be for an
individual in India by 2050, given projected food prices and specified
nutritional requirements?

## Methodology

The study employs **Linear Programming (LP)** and the **Simplex
Algorithm** to formulate and solve a cost-minimisation problem.

The model:

- Minimises the cost of a dietary basket comprising 14 food items.
- Incorporates nutritional requirements, including protein,
  carbohydrates, fats, salt and sugar constraints.
- Uses food-price data published by the Department of Consumer Affairs,
  Government of India.
- Projects food prices for 2050 using historical Consumer Price Index
  inflation data.
- Compares the affordability of the optimised dietary basket between
  2020 and 2050.

## Key Findings

The analysis estimates that the modelled vegan diet would remain
affordable at relatively low income levels in both periods. The minimum
income threshold identified by the model was approximately:

- **2020:** 0.08% of average per-capita income.
- **2050:** 1.9% of average per-capita income.

The dissertation also discusses the limitations of assessing dietary
affordability without accounting for future income distribution and
changes in food expenditure patterns.

## Methodological Approach

The research applies mathematical optimisation to an applied economic
problem, combining food-price data, nutritional constraints and
inflation-based price projections within a linear programming framework.
